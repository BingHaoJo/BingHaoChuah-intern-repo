What makes Focus Bear different from these apps?
- These apps specializes in one kind of main feature while Focus Bear inherits all the main features from these apps. These apps are able to expand on their one main features and add many quality of life feature to support the main feature. Focus Bear doesn't specialize in just one feature. Focus Bear have distraction blocking, habit routines, to do list and breaks. Each of the features is useful but doesn't expand on them too much.

If you were a user, why would you choose Focus Bear over competitors?
- I would choose Focus Bear because it has all of the main features from the competitors in one place. I can use the habit routine planning feature and use the focus session if I want to. I don't have to download two apps for different functions but I am not pressured to use one function over the other if I don't want to.

What's one feature that other apps have that Focus Bear doesn't?
- A cool feature I see from some of the competitor is that Tiimo and Routinery have a feature where users can setup a widget on their phone to see the tasks to do, streaks or focus timer. I think Focus Bear mobile app could benefit from that.

Based on your research, what's one improvement you think Focus Bear could make?
- One thing I found in common in all the competitor's product is that there are very simple to process and easy to use. Even just from the pictures alone, I can immediately tell what that function is. The other apps has one main feature so it would be easier to simplify. In Focus Bear, I had a hard time understanding what the different terms means, what the different function does and how to swiftly control the focus session, habit routine and breaks. It took me some time to fully understand the features but navigating around the app is not as intuitive as the other apps. There are tutorials but they are hidden behind 'Help & Support' which is at the bottom-left corner of the app. That makes it unlikely to be clicked. Average users especially those who have ADHD wouldn't care to click on tutorials, much less if they are hidden.
