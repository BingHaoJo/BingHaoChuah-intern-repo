What are best practices for handling confidential data?
- Identify and catalog what confidential data is in the database and where it resides.
- Encrypt data at rest and in transit.
- Use muti-factor authentication to access confidential accounts.
- Conduct regular security awareness training for employees.

How should you respond to a suspected data breach or accidental disclosure of confidential information?
- Disconnect compromised devices from the network, revoke unauthorized account access, or recall misdirected emails immediately.
- Do not shut down machines completely or delete logs; instead, take systems offline or snapshot them to retain forensic data for investigation.
-  Reset compromised passwords, API keys, or multi-factor authentication tokens.
-  Alert your privacy officer, IT department, legal counsel, and public relations lead.


Reflection

What steps can you take to ensure you handle data securely in your daily tasks?
- Use multi-factor authentication for personal and work accounts.
- Create unique password for every account and store them in a trusted password manager.
- Lock your computer, phone, or tablet every time you step away from your desk.
- Only use company-approved software, messaging apps, and cloud storage solutions to store and share data.

How should you store, share, and dispose of sensitive information safely?
- Keep digital files on company-approved cloud platforms or encrypted drives rather than your local desktop or unauthorized personal storage.
- Use secure file-transfer protocols (like HTTPS or SFTP) and share documents via password-protected, expiring links rather than email attachments.
- Permanently erase digital files using certified overwriting tools rather than just moving them to the "Recycle Bin."

What are some common mistakes that lead to data privacy issues, and how can they be avoided?
- Misdirected Communications such as sending emails, attachments, or links to the wrong person due to autocomplete or bulk-cc'ing. This can be avoided by using a "delayed send" rule to catch mistakes or use BCC for external distribution lists, or use dedicated email marketing tools.
- Shadow IT such as using unapproved personal apps, free AI tools, or public cloud storage to handle work data. This can be avoided by strictly using company-vetted platforms and never paste confidential data into public AI models or unencrypted text tools.
